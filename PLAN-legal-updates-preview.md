## 5. AI Protection: helping people show proof, while staying out of their disputes

Best practice is to say plainly what the certificate is **for** (evidence people can use), what it **isn't**
(ownership, a registration, a guarantee), and that the company **isn't a party** to anyone's dispute. You already
have the "isn't" part; this adds the purpose and the line that keeps the business out of personal situations.

**AI Protection disclaimer (Hub, certificates, verify page). Before:**
> A certificate is evidence that your files existed in this exact form by a date. It isn't proof of ownership, a
> copyright registration, legal advice or a guarantee against AI training, imitation, infringement, claims or
> takedowns, and courts decide how much weight it gets. The attestation is your own statement, so make sure it's
> true. For the strongest protection, register your work with the U.S. Copyright Office.

**After:**
> AI Protection certificates are made to help you show proof: evidence that your files existed in this exact form by
> a date, which you can include with takedown requests, platform disputes and legal claims. A certificate isn't proof
> of ownership, a copyright registration, legal advice or a guarantee against AI training, imitation, infringement,
> claims or takedowns, and courts and platforms decide how much weight it gets. The attestation is your own statement,
> so make sure it's true. Came Through Audio isn't a party to your disputes and doesn't investigate, take sides or act
> as a witness; anyone can check a certificate themselves on our verify page. For the strongest protection, register
> your work with the U.S. Copyright Office.

**Terms §11 gets the same two additions** (the "made to help you show proof" opening and the "isn't a party … verify
page" sentence), plus: "If we receive a valid subpoena or court order about a certificate, we respond as the law
requires."

## 6. Your question: does "Came Through" as a nickname need a DBA?

- **Listing nicknames in the Terms (§4 above) is unrelated to a DBA.** It just tells readers that "Came Through" and
  "Came Thru" mean Came Through Audio, LLC.
- **A DBA (in Georgia, a "trade name" registration, filed with the Clerk of Superior Court in the county where the
  business is) is needed when the business actually trades under a name that isn't its legal name:** signing
  contracts, invoicing, taking payments or opening a bank account as "Came Through" or "Came Thru".
- If "Came Through" is a nickname in marketing while contracts, invoices, Stripe and the bank all say Came Through
  Audio, LLC, a trade name filing generally isn't needed. If you plan to use "Came Through" on its own as a business
  name, file one. Confirm with your attorney or accountant; it's a small county filing.
