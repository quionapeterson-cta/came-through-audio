## 0. Principles

1. **Thank people for outcomes and quality, never hours.** No late-night, weekend or overtime awards for staff. The
   customer Night Shift badges stay customer-only (burnout and overtime-pay risk).
2. **Money follows profit.** Bonuses come from a pool that only exists when the company made a profit and kept its
   cash reserve. No profit, no pool, and everyone can see why.
3. **Badges are thanks, bonuses are math.** Badges are frequent, warm and never cost money by themselves. The bonus
   uses a written formula. A few top badges come with a small gift (§4).
4. **Earned from real records.** Every award and score comes from data in the app (accepted registrations, closed
   tickets, passed courses, resolved findings), granted by the server. Nobody grants themselves anything, the same rule as
   customer badges and paid access.
5. **Separation of duties.** Finance calculates the pool, the founder approves it, HR tells people. Staff see their own
   scorecard and the pool status, never anyone else's pay.

## 1. Department scorecards (what "doing well" means)

Each department gets 3 numbers, reviewed weekly. They drive team badges and the bonus. One of the three is always a
**quality** number, so rushing never pays.

| Department | Output | Quality (gate) | Customer / company result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Registration | Accepted registrations | Rejection rate under 5% | Days from Ready to registered |
| Royalty Accounts | Split sheets completed and signed | Disputes reopened under 2% | Payout questions answered within 2 business days |
| Finance | Books closed by the 5th | Reconciliation off by $0 | Paddle payouts matched to bank within 7 days |
| Branding | Assets delivered on time | Brand-rule audit pass rate (BRANDING.md) | Badge drop shipped every 6 months |
| Marketing & Social Media | Signups from campaigns | Honest-claims check passes (no false promises) | Trial → paid conversion from campaigns |
| Sales | New paid plans | 90-day retention of those customers | Revenue from new plans |
| Support | Tickets solved | Reopened under 5% | First reply under 4 business hours, satisfaction score |
| AI | AI Protection certificates processed | Timestamp confirmations with no errors | Turnaround from upload to certificate |
| Software | Releases shipped | Bugs that reached users per release | Uptime and page speed |
| Security | Security findings closed within target time | No critical finding open over 7 days | Quarterly access review done on time |
| HR | Roles filled | New hires still here after 90 days | Training and 30-60-90 completion |

Targets start as these suggestions and are set each quarter by the founder with each lead.

## 4. Bonus plan (tied to real profit)

### Step 1: has the bonus program started?
The pool program turns on only when **both** are true (founder, 2026-10-20):
- The company has earned **$1,000,000 in total net profit** since launch, after taxes and every other expense
  (payroll, contractors, vendors, the founder's pay), and
- Profit is **growing steadily**: the last 12 months' net profit beats the 12 months before, at most one of the last
  four quarters went down, and Finance's projection for the next four quarters doesn't go down.

### Step 2: is there a pool this quarter?
Once the program is on, a quarter has a pool only when **both** are true at quarter close:
- Net profit for the quarter is above $0, and
- Cash on hand still covers **3 months of expenses** after paying the pool.

Staff see a progress bar toward the $1,000,000 start line ("pool starts at $1M profit"), not the profit figures.

### Step 3: how big is the pool?
**Pool = 10% of quarterly net profit**, but each person's bonus is capped by their level (founder, 2026-10-20). Anything
over the caps stays in the company.

| Level | Cap (% of their base pay for the quarter) |
| --- | --- |
| Specialist I (new hires, first year) | 1% |
| Specialist II | 2% |
| Senior Specialist | 3% |
| Lead | 4% |
| Department Lead / senior leadership | 5% |

Levels follow the career ladder (PLAN-operations §2): moving up takes time in the role **and** the certifications and
scorecard results for that level, so seniority means experience plus quality, not years alone.

**Raising the caps toward 15%:** the founder reviews the caps once a year. A raise happens only when the last four
quarters were all profitable, cash covers 6 months of expenses, and the pool was cut down by the caps in at least two of
those quarters (meaning the company could afford more). Suggested steps: 1–5% → 2–7% → 4–10% → 6–15%. Caps never drop
mid-quarter, and any change is announced before the quarter starts.

### Step 4: who gets what
- **Half the pool is the team share.** It is split among departments that met their quality gate. Each department's
  piece is its headcount share × its scorecard result (0 to 1.2). Inside the department it's split equally.
- **Half the pool is the individual share.** Each person's points = their scorecard result (0 to 1.2) × FTE (1.0 full
  time, 0.5 half time). Pay = their points ÷ everyone's points × the individual half.
- **Quality gate:** if a department misses its quality number, its team share for that quarter is $0 and individual
  results are capped at 0.8. This stops "fast but wrong".
- **Eligibility:** employed through the quarter's last day and not on a performance plan. New hires join after their
  90-day mark, prorated.

### Worked example (made-up numbers)
(Ignores the level caps, to show the split. With caps, each payout is cut to its cap and the rest stays in the company.)
Quarter net profit $40,000 → pool $4,000 → $2,000 team + $2,000 individual.
Registration (2 people) met its quality gate with a result of 1.1; Support (1 person) met it with 1.0. Team pieces:
Registration 2 × 1.1 = 2.2 parts, Support 1 × 1.0 = 1.0 part → Registration $1,375 ($687.50 each), Support $625.
Individual points: 1.2 + 1.0 + 1.0 = 3.2 → $750, $625, $625.

### Payment and paperwork
- Paid with payroll about 45 days after quarter close, once the books are closed.
- **Spot gifts:** $25–$100 for Platinum staff badges and the quarterly Came Through Award. They start once total net
  profit passes **$1,200,000**, funded by an **$18,000 five-year budget** ($3,600 a year, $300 a month cap; unused money
  rolls to the next month within the same year). Gift cards count as taxable wages; Finance runs them through payroll.
- **Contractors** (regional registration experts) get their quality bonus through their contract or employer-of-record
  platform, not this pool (PLAN-operations §5).
- Written plan document that each person signs: the formula, the gates, that the company may change the plan for future
  quarters, and that a bonus isn't earned until approved.
- **Check with your accountant / employment lawyer (Georgia):** bonuses for hourly (non-exempt) staff must be added to
  their regular rate when overtime is calculated (FLSA nondiscretionary bonus rule); payroll tax on gifts; the
  "employed on payout date" rule.

## 7. Legal points to review with an employment lawyer and accountant (Georgia) before the first payout

This is a checklist of common issues, not legal advice.

- **Write it down.** A signed plan document: the formula, the $1M start line, the gates, when a bonus counts as earned
  (only after founder approval), that the company can change the plan for future quarters, and what happens if someone
  leaves before payout. Unclear plans are the most common source of bonus disputes.
- **Overtime (FLSA).** A formula bonus is "nondiscretionary". For hourly (non-exempt) staff, it must be added to their
  regular rate, and overtime for that quarter recalculated. Payroll software can do this; it has to be switched on.
- **Same rules for everyone.** Apply the formula the same way to every person, use objective scorecard numbers, and keep the
  records. Scorecards must not penalize protected leave (medical, family, military, disability accommodations): prorate
  by time worked under one written rule rather than lowering scores.
- **Employed on the payout date.** Usually allowed when it's written in the plan; confirm for Georgia and for any
  staff in other states or countries.
- **Taxes.** Bonuses and gift cards are wages: federal supplemental withholding (22% flat option), Georgia withholding,
  Social Security and Medicare. Gift cards are never a tax-free "gift" for employees.
- **Timing.** Paying within 2½ months after the year a bonus is earned keeps it out of deferred-compensation rules (IRC
  409A). The 45-day schedule fits.
- **Contractors stay out of the employee pool.** Paying contractors employee-style bonuses can look like they're really
  employees (misclassification). They get the quality bonus in their own contracts.
- **Staff in other countries.** Some countries treat a bonus paid several times in a row as a right. Use the
  employer-of-record platform's local terms.
- **Pay talk is allowed.** Profit numbers can stay confidential, but staff can't be told not to discuss their own pay or
  bonus with each other (National Labor Relations Act).
- **Multi-department staff.** The plan names one home department per person for the team share.

## 9. Hiring model: 1099 contractors, employees, or both?

**Recommendation: a mix, contractor-first for project work, employees for the core daily jobs, and an employer-of-record
platform for anyone outside the US.** Not legal advice; confirm with the employment lawyer.

The key point: **you don't get to pick the label.** The IRS and the Department of Labor look at how the work is done. If
CTA controls when, where and how someone works, they are usually an employee, whatever the contract says.

| Fits a 1099 contractor | Fits an employee (W-2) |
| --- | --- |
| A defined result: badge art, a marketing campaign, a software feature, a legal review | Ongoing daily work that is the core of the business: Support, Registration |
| They choose their hours, tools and method | CTA sets hours, SOPs, training, scorecards and the time clock |
| They have other clients and invoice CTA | They work mainly or only for CTA |
| Paid per project or invoice | Paid wages; in the bonus pool |

**What this means for the plans we've written:** scorecards, required Training, quality sampling, the time clock and the
bonus pool are all signs of control. Use them for employees. Contractors get a clear statement of work, deadlines and a
quality bonus written into their contract, and can earn staff badges as thanks (no money attached).

**Startup path:**
1. **Now (pre-launch):** contractors for project work (art, software sprints, campaigns, legal). Founder handles Support
   and Registration.
2. **First steady daily role** (likely Support or Registration, once there is regular weekly volume): hire it as a
   part-time **employee** through a payroll service (e.g. Gusto) or a PEO (e.g. Justworks). Costs to budget: about 7.65%
   employer Social Security and Medicare, federal and Georgia unemployment tax, and workers' comp (Georgia requires it at 3
   or more employees).
3. **Regional registration experts abroad:** never 1099 (that form is US-only). Use an employer-of-record or contractor
   platform (Deel, Remote, Oyster) as PLAN-operations §5 says.
4. **Bonus pool:** employees only, from the day the $1,000,000 start line is reached.

**Why not 1099 for everyone at first:** it's cheaper on paper, but if a "contractor" is really doing an employee's job,
CTA can owe back payroll taxes, penalties, unpaid overtime and benefits, and the person can lose unemployment and
workers' comp protection. It is one of the most common and expensive startup mistakes.
